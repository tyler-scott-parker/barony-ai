A mod for Barony · v5.0.2

Adorcism

adorcism n. — the deliberate invitation of a spirit into a body, so that it wakes and acts alongside you. The inverse of exorcism. Barony’s own word: the Adorcise menu binds a spirit into a weapon so it fights at your side.

Everything in the dungeon can speak, and means it. Followers, townsfolk, merchants, the named dead — all of them talk through a language model running on your own machine, grounded in Barony’s actual lore, and they remember what you did. Nothing leaves your computer. There is no account, no API key, and no bill.

Compendium

What it sounds like

Every line below came out of the model during development, unedited. Nothing here is scripted, and none of it is in the base game — vanilla Barony gives spoken dialogue to humans only.

A dummybotDeployed
“Bring it on, Sparkle Face!”

Shouted at a crystal golem. A dummybot is a training dummy on a spring — its entire combat value is being noticed — so it heckles whatever is nearest, constantly, and is certain it is a celebrated warrior.

A skeleton knightConjured
“Three times, I think. You unmade me and called me back each time. You are a peculiar one, Ada.”

Asked how many times it had died. A Conjurer’s summons keep one identity across every recast, so the count is real rather than invented.

A goblin followerResentful
“Fine, you want me to go first? I’m not getting killed again because of your recklessness.”

Same order, same character, after a run where they had been hurt and left behind. They obeyed — resentfully. On a clean run the same follower answers “Aye, boss!”

A human companionHiding something
“Family’s not something I’m comfortable talkin’ about.”

Not every recruit is loyal. This one deflected a question about their past and then asked, unprompted, about the player’s rations and route.

A sentrybotFixed in place
“I’ll hold this spot, you go ahead. I’ve got my eye on the corridor.”

Asked to come along. A deployed turret cannot move, and it will tell you so rather than promising something it cannot do.

A shopkeeperNot interested
“I’m a shopkeeper, not a soldier.”

Asked to join the party. Merchants greet you inside the shop window when you open it, and they are not recruitable no matter how the request is phrased.

Systems

What it actually does

It is not a chat box bolted to the side of the game. Conversation feeds systems that change how the run goes, and none of them ever show you a number.

Everyone speaks

All 49 creatures the game can produce, plus merchants, townsfolk and the roughly thirty named characters — each with its own written sense of what it is and what it could plausibly know.

They remember

Fighting beside you, being healed, being wounded, being struck by you, being left behind, watching another companion die. Recorded as events, recalled specifically — one follower brought up “the last time you left me behind, floor 7” without being asked.

Seven kinds of regard

Friendship, trust, respect, fear, resentment, dependence, curiosity — tracked separately, so a companion can like you without trusting you, or obey you out of fear. You never see the numbers; you hear the difference.

Obedience is earned

A refusal is a real refusal, not flavour text over compliance. Respect without warmth reads as purely tactical; fear that outweighs friendship reads as obedience a little too quick and a little too flat.

Not everyone is loyal

A small share of recruits are working against you, with a reason of their own. They deflect on their past and take too much interest in your route. One who comes to like you anyway will eventually strain, and let something slip.

The Baron’s weakness

A companion you have earned may tell you something true about Baron Herx that measurably weakens him. A companion who is deceiving you may tell you something plausible and wrong. You find out which at the boss.

“What is this thing?”

Hand a companion an unidentified item. Whether they know is one question; whether they tell you the truth is another. A confident lie leaves you carrying something that is not what you were told.

Gifts, warnings, sabotage

Companions who like you occasionally volunteer something — a scrap of food, a piece of local knowledge, or quietly disarming the traps on the floor. Rare on purpose.

They name themselves

Get close enough and a companion tells you its name, and the party display starts using it. Names come from a pool of over 1,400 written per species, and one you have met will not come back in your next run.

You are not always understood

Polymorph into a rat and most of the dungeon stops making sense — you get chittering and snarling instead of speech, unless the speaker is close enough to your own kind. Turn back and the words return.

Class companions differ

A Conjurer’s summons carry one relationship across every recast and remember being unmade. A Machinist’s turrets know they are bolted down. Charmed creatures are dungeon natives with histories of their own.

Speak and be spoken to Optional

Hold a key and talk to your companion instead of typing. Turn on the other half and the dungeon answers out loud, in 109 distinct voices cast by species. Both off by default.

Requirements

Who needs what

In co-op, only the host runs anything extra. Everyone else installs the mod and joins. The setup burden falls on one person by design.

You areYou installYou need
Hosting The mod, Python 3, Ollama, and one 8B model A GPU with 8 GB of memory or better. Developed on an RTX 2070 Super; replies come back in about 2–4 seconds and the model sits entirely on the card.
Joining The mod. Nothing else. Nothing. No Python, no model, no port forwarding, no configuration. Your companions talk because the host’s machine is doing the work.
Playing alone Same as hosting Same as hosting.

The default model is llama3.1:8b. If you would rather point it at something else — a bigger local model, or a machine down the hall — /aiserver <url> in the console redirects it, and every setting has an environment variable.

Setup · host only

Getting it running

  1. Install Ollama and pull the model

    Ollama is a free local model runner. Install it, then pull the model once — about 5 GB, downloaded a single time.

    ollama pull llama3.1:8b

  2. Install the mod

    TODO: install path and file list, once the release build exists.

  3. Start the dialogue service

    Leave this running in the background while you play. It talks only to your own machine.

    python3 service.py

  4. Play

    Talk to a companion with /aicommand <what you want to say>, or click any townsperson or merchant to start a conversation. Ask a companion about an item with /aiidentify. If something goes wrong, /ailog <what happened> writes a note into the session timeline so it can be traced afterwards.

  5. Optional: voice

    Hold a key and speak instead of typing, and / or have the dungeon answer out loud. Both halves are off unless you turn them on, and neither competes with the model for graphics memory.

    python3 voice_bridge.py                    # speak to them
python3 tts_bridge.py --setup              # one time, ~80 MB
BARONY_AI_TTS=1 python3 service.py         # and hear them
Honest status

Before release

Written down plainly because a mod page that oversells is worse than one that waits. Everything described above is built and works on the development machine. These are the things standing between that and a Nexus download.

  • Blocker

    Windows support. The mod and the service pass messages through files under /tmp, which is a Linux path, and the book-lore reader still assumes a Linux Steam install. Both need a portability pass before anyone on Windows can run it.

  • Blocker

    A full playthrough. Every system has been verified in isolation, but no one has yet played a whole run start to finish. The tuning — how often gifts appear, whether a deceiver is catchable in the moment, whether the Baron’s secret is reachable before you reach him — is educated guesswork until then.

  • Blocker

    Co-op on two machines. The multiplayer path is written and verified one side at a time, but a second computer has never joined. Four people talking at once queue on one graphics card, and how that feels is unknown.

  • Ready

    Everything else. Lore for all 49 species, the relationship and memory systems, the non-follower conversations, item identification, class companions, optional voice in and out, and a session log that makes a bad run diagnosable afterwards.

Credits

Standing on other people’s work

Barony

By Turning Wheel LLC, and open source — which is the only reason a mod like this can exist at all. Adorcism is built against v5.0.2 and changes as little of the game as it can.

Ollama

Does the heavy lifting. Every word your companions say is generated locally by Ollama, on your own hardware, with nothing sent anywhere. The mod would not be practical without it.

Llama 3.1, Whisper, Piper

The default model, the speech recognition behind push-to-talk, and the voices behind spoken replies. All local, all optional except the first.

Inspired by the AI-companion mods that came before it in other games — CHIM, Mantella and Herika for Skyrim chief among them — which proved the idea was worth building and set the bar for what these characters should sound like.
